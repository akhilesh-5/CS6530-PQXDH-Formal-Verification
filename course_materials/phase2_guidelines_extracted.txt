Final project deliverables 
Submit the following three artefacts. The report, technical artefacts, video and presentation must describe the same 
completed work. 
Artefact 1  Completed technical report 
The report must consolidate the understanding, analysis, experiments, evidence and engineering conclusions 
obtained from the project. It should clearly capture the outcomes of Activities 3 to 6 specified for the assigned 
project theme, while retaining the necessary context from Activities 1 and 2. 
• Problem statement, primary-paper understanding and security objective 
• Relevant system, protocol or security model and the threat or design analysis 
• Analysis of the security failure and relevant supporting literature 
• Configuration and experimental environment 
• Reproduction, experiment or technical-study procedure 
• Baseline result and supporting evidence 
• Modified exploration and the resulting behaviour 
• Selected logs, screenshots or outputs that support the findings 
• Security Evolution Matrix 
• Four or five engineering lessons 
• Limitations, difficulties and deviations from the paper or prescribed experiment 
• Brief statement of each member's contribution 
• Brief declaration of generative-AI usage 
Evidence must be selective and relevant. Do not include raw or excessive logs merely for completeness. 
Artefact 2  Code scripts models and configuration 
Submit the student-developed code, scripts, formal models, configuration files and relevant modifications used for 
the technical study, as applicable to the project. Clearly distinguish:

Slide requirements 
• A maximum of four content slides is permitted. A minimal title slide may be added and does not count toward the 
four content slides. 
• The four content slides must collectively capture the six activities specified for the project theme. 
• The slides must be consistent with the submitted report, technical artefacts and demonstration video. 
• Use selected evidence and results. Do not overload the slides with text, logs or screenshots. 
• Any video excerpt shown during the presentation counts within the 12-minute presentation time. 
Slide submission 
Upload the slides to the course Moodle page by 10:00 PM IST on the day before the team's assigned presentation. 
The submitted version will be copied to the classroom system and treated as the official presentation version. A 
replacement may be accepted only to correct a genuine factual, rendering or technical error, with prior instructor 
approval. 
Speaking order and participation 
For a three-member team, three slips numbered 1, 2 and 3 will be used. Each member will randomly pick one slip, 
and the team will present in that order. Every member must present at least one content slide and must be prepared 
to explain the complete project. The team may plan how the fourth content slide is shared or assigned. Two-member 
teams must ensure that both members present; a single-member team will present the complete work. 
The questions after the presentation are for clarification. They are not the individual project viva.

Individual written viva 
A 30-minute individual written viva will be conducted on Friday, 6 November 2026, after the final team presentation. 
Every student will answer questions about the student's own project. The assessment will cover: 
• Project security objective and threat model 
• Cryptographic or key-flow explanation 
• One negative test and what its result demonstrated 
• One failed assumption, limitation or implementation difficulty 
• A project-specific question requiring the student to defend a design decision or proposed improvement 
Questions 1 to 4 will have a common structure but must be answered using the student's own project. Question 5 will 
have team-coded variants. The written viva assesses individual understanding; marks may therefore differ among 
members of the same team.

Contingency arrangements 
The scheduled assessment mode will be used whenever possible. If an officially recognized disruption or documented 
illness prevents that mode, an equivalent alternative will be used without increasing the project scope. Students will 
not lose marks solely because an assessment could not be conducted for reasons outside their control.

Severe weather or institute-wide disruption 
• A cancelled presentation will be rescheduled within the remaining presentation periods wherever feasible. 
• If rescheduling is not feasible, the submitted four-slide deck and demonstration video will be used, together with a 
short narrated explanation in which every available team member participates. A second full technical 
demonstration will not be required. 
• The individual written viva will be conducted in the earliest feasible common physical or timed online slot. If 
neither is possible before marks must be finalized, an equivalent individual project-specific written assessment 
will be announced. 
• The submission deadline will remain unchanged when Moodle and normal connectivity remain available. If an 
officially announced disruption materially affects access, one common revised deadline will be announced for all 
affected teams. 
Illness affecting a student or team 
• The student or team must inform the instructor at the earliest feasible time and provide the documentation 
required under institute policy. 
• If one member is ill, the team should normally submit and present as scheduled. The absent member's 
presentation contribution may be assessed from the submitted evidence or through a brief makeup interaction, 
and a written-viva makeup will be arranged where feasible. 
• If the entire team is affected, the instructor will determine an appropriate short extension, rescheduled 
presentation or equivalent assessment using the submitted work. 
• The contingency provisions apply only to officially recognized disruption, documented illness or another reason 
accepted under institute policy. Unexplained absence may result in loss of the corresponding assessment marks.
